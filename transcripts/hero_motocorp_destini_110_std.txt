The new Hero Destini 110 was launched in September last year, but we didn't really get a chance to ride it then. Almost 6 months later, we finally have the scooter with us and it's here to answer an important question. Now, currently, the 110cc family scooter segment is dominated by the Honda Activa and the TVS Jupiter. And we're here to find out how this one stacks up against those two scooters and how it fares as an everyday commuter. The 110cc engine in this scooter puts out 8.1 horsepower and around 8.7 Nm of peak torque. Out in the real world, the acceleration is linear and the throttle response is quite gentle and that will make this really easy for newer riders or older riders to manage. Now, this builds up speed progressively and ultimately out in the real world, that means that it is a very predictable and unintimidating scooter to ride. The scooter is in its sweet spot between 30 and 60 km/h. That's where it feels the most natural. You It does even hit higher speeds and can cruise at highway speeds as well, but when you're out on the highway, overtakes are going to require a little more planning. Overall, the Destini 110 feels good enough for daily commutes, delivering its performance in a peaceful manner. In our test, the performance was almost on par with the Activa and the Jupiter. Up to 40 km/h, both scooters have a very slight acceleration advantage over the Destini, but as it builds speed, it took around 18 seconds to get from 0 to 80 km/h. In our fuel efficiency test, we were able to get around 54 km/L in the city and around 64 km/L out on the highway, making this quite a fuel-efficient scooter, but do keep in mind that the mileage will vary depending on your riding style and riding conditions. Now, in terms of design, as you can see, it has design which is exactly similar to the new Destini 125. There are some visible panel gaps, so the overall fit and finish quality and attention to detail can stand to improve as it doesn't match its top rivals in this category. Now, here's how I fit on the Destini 110. As you can see, I cannot completely flat foot to the ground, but that's because this is a wide seat. It is also one of the heaviest scooters in the segment with a curb weight of 114 kg. This makes it approximately 10 kg heavier than the Activa and Jupiter, but it isn't an issue to handle at a standstill or on the go. The way Hero MotoCorp have tuned the suspension of the scooter is actually one of the best parts about it. Feature-wise, the Destiny keeps things simple with a digi-analog instrument cluster displaying a trip meter, odometer and real-time fuel efficiency alongside the analog speedometer. Underseat storage is on the smaller side but it can accommodate a half-face helmet along with a few essentials. Up front, there's a large storage pocket that easily fits in a water bottle and a type-A USB charging port sits conveniently above it. The 5.3 L fuel tank gets an external fuel filler cap which is a practical touch for daily use. Hero's i3s idle start-stop system worked fairly well in traffic. The engine cuts off after a few seconds at a signal and restarts smoothly with the twist of the throttle while engaging the brake. Only on rare occasions did it hesitate slightly before firing back to life. It unfortunately misses out on any connectivity features which might be a deal breaker for some customers. The Destiny 110 is priced at 72,000 rupees for the base VX variant and goes up to 79,750 rupees for the top spec ZX variant. This makes it around 10,000 rupees more affordable than the top variant of the Activa and 7,000 rupees less than the top spec variant of the Jupiter. Overall, the Destini 110 is a simple and easy to ride scooter, and what it has going for it is really good ride quality and comfort, good mileage, and a price that undercuts the Activa and the Jupiter. That said, it does miss out on connectivity features, and while it is more affordable, the quality levels clearly feel like it was built to undercut its rivals on price.

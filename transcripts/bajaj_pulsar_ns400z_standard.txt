Early last year, Baj la la la la la la la la la la la la la la la la la la la launched the Pulsar NS 400C. It grabbed a lot of attention for offering some serious performance at an aggressive price point. It was the most powerful Pulsar ever. However, it wasn't void of its fair share of flaws. Now though, Baj has rolled out a comprehensive update, one that claims to fix all the weak points of the NS 400Z. So, does this 400 cc Street fight to live up to its full potential? Let's find out. [Music] [Applause] Visually, not much has changed, and honestly, that's not a bad thing. The NS-1400Z still carries an aggressive stance with its sharp rake angle, compact proportions, and signature ZD DRL. The mono projector headlamp gives it some individuality. The tail section feels a little too familiar, nearly identical to the NS-200's. Feature- wise, things stay strong. There's right by wire, traction control, four riding modes, and a new birectional quick shifter. Now, instead of using a dedicated sensor system like on most quick shifters, Bajage has done something clever. They have repurposed the standard gear position sensor and made it work in tandem with the gear drum. The result they say is software enabled clutchless shifts both up and down at no additional hardware cost. It works impressively well at higher revs. Shifts are timely, downshifts are clean, but at lower RPMs it can feel a little clunky, which is why Bajage has limited it to sport mode. Now, this is where things are properly improved. One of the bigger gripes with the older NS400Z was grip. that's been addressed with the new Apollo H1 radial tires and a wider 150 section rear. The difference is immediate. The bike feels more confident midc corner, more planted, and you no longer second guessess your line. The 43 mm USD fork handles bumps well, though the rear shock is still a bit stiff, especially over continuous undulations. Under hard braking, the front also dives a bit more than ideal. Essentially, this is still relatively simple suspension, but given the bike's price, one can't really complain. But overall, the handling is sharp, the bike feels flickable, and despite the move to radials, as well as a few other upgrades, the NS400Z still weighs the same 174 kg. Performance- wise, Bajage has made real progress. The 373 cc engine now makes 43HP in sport mode, up from 40. The throttle response is crisper. The red line is 1,000 RPM higher, and the claimed 0 to 100 time has dropped by over a second. It still isn't KTM aggressive, but feels quicker and more alive, finally matching what the spec sheet promises. Other modes like road and rain retain the older 40hp tune and are better suited for calmer riding. Despite the extra power, claimed efficiency hasn't changed. Still at 28 kmppl. In real world terms, it's likely to stay close to what we saw before, just under 28 in the city and close to 30 on the highway. So, the question prevails, is the NS4NZ finally a complete allrounder? The answer is yes. For just rs 8,000 more, you get radial tires, you get a wider rear tire, you get better brakes, you get a more aggressive map and clutchless shift system. At rupes 1.92 lakh, this Pulsar NS400Z is one of the most well-rounded packages you can buy. And this really can be your entry in the 400cc at the price of a 250.

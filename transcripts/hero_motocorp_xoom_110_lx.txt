This is the all new Hero Xoom, the company's latest entrant into the hugely popular 110 cc scooter segment. Now, Hero already does have the Maestro Edge and the Pleasure Plus in this segment, but this one is a more sporty scooter that's been targeted towards the youth. Just how good is it? Let's take a look. But, before we do that, don't forget to subscribe to the Autocar India channel and click the bell icon to get notified every time we make a new upload. Now, let's start off by talking about the design. Now, one thing prospective buyers are sure to like is this scooter's striking design. It's angular and gets a lot of cuts and creases. Up front, you get this nice large LED headlamp with the H-shaped LED DRL, and higher up you get the indicators integrated into the handlebar cowl with a small flyscreen. Now, over to the side where usually we are used to seeing plain Jane side panels on other scooters, this one gets cuts and creases which appears a little more angular and attractive. Hero has also integrated the grab rails into the body, which looks a little nice. And to round off the look, it gets a H-shaped LED tail lamp as well. You have to give credit to Hero's design team as this is one of the best-looking 110 cc scooters on the market. And it is also very well equipped as well. We have the range-topping ZX variant on test here, and it gets all the bells and whistles. Aside from the LED headlamp and tail lamp, Hero has given the Xoom a segment-first feature in the form of cornering lights. This feature makes use of a gyroscope and an accelerometer, and depending on the lean angle, it lights up an LED lamp in the fairing in the direction of which you're turning. While we did get a demo of this feature and it worked well, we'll have to wait and see how effective it really is in the real world. Other features on the Zoom include a digital instrument cluster, Bluetooth connectivity, alloy wheels, and a USB charging port. Although the placement of the port is a bit awkward. While it does get all these features, Hero has not given the Zoom a multi-function key slot and an external fuel filler, which is a rather important convenience to miss. So, in order to tank up, you have to take the key out, open the boot, and then fill fuel, which is a bit of an old-school inconvenience. Overall, the Hero Xoom's ergonomics can be described as comfortable. I'm 5 ft 11 in tall and it's very comfortable for even my height. There isn't a long reach to the handlebar and the seat is nice and comfy and well-padded as well. There's ample of space on the floorboard, too. But, one thing you have to keep in mind that when turning from lock to lock, it does tend to foul a bit with your knee, which can be easily solved by putting your knee out of the way a little bit. However, this will be an issue only faced by riders who are 5 ft 10 in tall or more. In terms of storage spaces, the Hero Xoom has got you covered. You get 19.2 L of under-seat storage, two deep pockets in the front apron, which can hold 1-L bottles, and a hook to keep your grocery bags secure on the move. The build quality of the scooter is good to a large extent, but there is some room for improvement in certain areas. Things like the switchgear and mirrors could be made of higher-quality feeling materials. Powering the Hero is a 110 cc engine that puts out a little over 8 horsepower and 8.7 Newton meters of torque. Now, while we have seen this engine in other Hero scooters, the company says this has been specifically tuned for this application. Now, out on the road, it does feel peppy enough, but it can't really be described as fast. And in terms of refinement, there is a mild buzziness that creeps in at lower speeds, but as soon as you pick up the speed and cross about 50 to 60 kph, it becomes really smooth. And in terms of fuel efficiency, while we haven't tested it, it is said to be one of the most fuel-efficient scooters in the segment, and it also comes with Hero's i3s technology. i3s is Hero speak for an auto start-stop system. This fuel-saving tech cuts the engine off when you come to a halt and then starts it up when you press the brake lever and twist the throttle. While it is a nice inclusion, this system isn't as seamless to operate as Suzuki's version on the new Burgman Street EX. It takes a good few seconds to cut off, and having to press the brake and then use the throttle to set off seems a bit counterintuitive. The Hero Zoom also impresses in the ride and handling department. Now, this scooter runs on 12-in wheels at both ends, which provides it with good stability around corners. The ride is also very well judged, and it deals with bumps with relative ease. While we did ride it on relatively smooth roads out here in Delhi NCR, we'll wait and see how it deals with our pothole-ridden streets in Mumbai. Hero says the Zoom gets a new design for the frame, different from its other 110 cc offerings, as this one has been designed specifically for its 12-in wheel setup at both ends. As for the braking, this top spec ZX variant gets a disc brake up front and a drum at the rear. The braking performance is good with decent feedback. The Hero Xoom does come across as a well-rounded package. It has an attractive design, a lot of features, and decent performance, as well. Priced from rupees 68,600, the Hero Xoom range starts on par with rival, the Honda Dio. Sure, the Xoom's ZX variants cost around rupees 4,000 more than the Dio at rupees 76,700, but then it is far better equipped, as well. Judging by initial impressions, the Hero Xoom does come across as a promising scooter. So, if you are on the lookout for a sporty 110-cc scooter, this one is definitely worth taking a look at.
